## Minutes
1. **Minutes approved**: for 13 July & 29 June

2. **Co-Lead elected**: Rachel Foucard (TYPO3) was confirmed as Co-Lead alongside Dave Russo (Red Hat).

3. **Meetings are 55 mins**: Our “1 hour” meetings will always aim to end 5 mins early, to avoid back-to-back calls.

4. **Project contributor policies**: We discussed the idea of analysing the new wave of policies that projects are adopting for accepting contributions which include AI generated code.

   1. Many were not sure this is sufficiently relevant to the SIG’s focus on compliance.

   2. We agreed that some people may look into the topic to raise it again in the future.

   3. It was noted that the discussion could be helpful even if we don’t have a target deliverable and that we may send comments to the Sovereign AI Foundation to ask if they wish to discuss it.

5. **Level 2 & 3 measures of the AI Act**: To gauge interest, we briefly looked at a list of ten follow-on measures the EC is working on. The ten measures are listed after these minutes.

   1. **CRA interaction**: it was noted that there seems to be a low level of awareness among AI projects that the CRA applies to them.

      1. Everyone agrees the CRA applies to the software around the model. Need to confirm how CRA applies to models (are the guardrails in them “software”? Does the CRA obligation to be secure require effective guardrails?).

      2. This might be a useful topic to clarify in a FAQ.

      3. Worth thinking about “AI BOM”, to check with Sovereign AI Foundation.

      4. We need to coordinate with the ORC CR group and minimise duplicate discussions.

   2. **GPAI Code of Practice**: There was interest in the status, worth getting more information for a future call. There is interest in analysis of when it is good or bad to sign the GPAI Code of Practice.

6. **EC high-risk AI consultation**: This is now closed. Eclipse Foundation submitted comments.

7. **AI Act standards on cybersecurity**: No official comments submitted as ORC (or EF) but we sent comments to some people who are involved.  This work is made difficult by Cen/Cenelec policy, or uncertainty on our side, against sharing of Cen/Cenelec drafts.

8. **AOB**:
   1. We may need more discussion of how to fulfil the CRA’s security review requirements in the new context of code being written by AI agents.  Need to think about coordination with the ORC CR SIG.
