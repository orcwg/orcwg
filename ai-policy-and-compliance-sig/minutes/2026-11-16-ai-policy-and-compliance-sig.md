## Notes

**Operationalising deliverables: FAQ**

The group agreed to formally propose an AI Act FAQ as a SIG deliverable to the ORC Steering Committee. The Eclipse Foundation (Ciaran O'Riordan and Javier Valino) will lead the editorial effort, with several members volunteering to contribute. The SIG will replicate the established structure, methodology, and GitHub-based workflow previously used for the Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) FAQ. Participants emphasized that formulating precise questions and discussing them openly on GitHub is critical, and while LLMs can assist in structuring content, human subject-matter expertise remains essential for accuracy.

**Operationalising deliverables: Inventory**

The SIG agreed to formally propose an AI Act Resource Inventory as a deliverable to the ORC Steering Committee, with Rachel Fouchard serving as the lead. The inventory will adopt the same structure and template used for the successful CRA Inventory. Members noted that categorizing resources will be challenging due to the complex intersection of AI and cybersecurity, suggesting that leveraging existing ENISA guidance and EU Commission documentation will help establish a shared vocabulary and organizational framework.

**Operationalising deliverables: CRA/AI Act overlap**

The group agreed to propose a document analyzing the overlap between the CRA and the AI Act as a formal deliverable to the ORC Steering Committee. Juan Rico and Sankalp Gilda will co-lead the effort, which is currently undergoing revision. Initial drafts highlighted critical intersections, particularly around Article 12, but members raised significant concerns regarding the accuracy of LLM-generated structural outlines. The group decided to manually refine the document’s foundational skeleton to ensure it correctly reflects the nuanced legal realities of the legislation before proceeding with detailed drafting. 

**Plan for Code and Compliance, pre-FOSDEM edition**

The upcoming pre-FOSDEM edition of the Code and Compliance event will focus heavily on interactive, hands-on workshops rather than standard presentations. The organizers plan to divide the expected attendees into smaller roundtable groups (approximately 10 people each) to tackle specific regulatory challenges. Proposed workshop topics include the interaction between the CRA and AI Act, defining "Free and Open Source AI" under the AI Act, mapping the AI Act's overlap with non-CRA legislation, and comparing global AI regulations (such as those in California and the UK).
